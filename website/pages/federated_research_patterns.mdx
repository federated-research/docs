# Federated Research Patterns

<Callout type="default">
    These concepts are still emerging, so get [in touch](mailto:healthinformatics@nottingham.ac.uk) if you have feedback.
</Callout>

There are numerous ways to undertake federated analytics and federated learning. There are also many ways to formally describe the types of analyses
that are to be performed especially via formal mathematical definitions. 

We frame the domain of federated analytics and federated learning via three concepts. 

## Possible 
The domain focuses on what analyses are possible to be performed under different federated scenarios and therefore is a more technical representation of the various platforms and algorithms. This concept captures a rapidly developing technology ecosystem.

## Acceptable 
Our focus of work is in the use of the possible platforms within and between trusted research environments, analysing data that is considered to be sensitive. These environments tend to be heavily controlled and locked down. This concept focuses on the areas to understand what possible federated methods are likely to be acceptable, and based on what criteria would a secure environment make those decisions. Therefore, not every federated approach that is possible is acceptable to these highly secure environments. 

## Useful 
The other concept is to understand the utility of the approaches for the researcher. This is to help clarify what analyses are possible when utilising a possible platform that is acceptable. Not every technology approach provides utility to researchers, but there is also a risk in adjusting the possible to make it more acceptable we lose the usefulness to the research community.  

The aim of this work in creating platforms, is to have a neutral language that simplifies the inherrent technmical and governance complexities to aid the conversation so that we can have initial conversations that transcend the three concepts of Possible, Acceptable, Useful.
