# What standards are we adopting?

The standards listed represent key standards adopted by UK-wide research programmes, including HDR UK and DARE UK, and are internationally recognised across the sensitive data research and research data infrastructure ecosystem.  

### Five Safes
A set of principles to describe how decisions about granting access to data should be made: 
- Safe People 
- Safe Data, 
- Safe Project, 
- Safe Outputs, 
- Safes Settings 

Widely adopted principles across the research data infrastructure ecosystem and a core mechanism across most domains of research. Click here to read more on the [Five Safes](https://ukdataservice.ac.uk/help/secure-lab/what-is-the-five-safes-framework/).

### Findable Accessible Interoperable Reusable (FAIR)
A set of principles that describes guidelines to improve transparency, reproducibility, and reusability of data and metadata:
- Findable
- Accessible
- Reusable
- Interoperable

FAIR has garnered significant adoption with the research community and has become a requirement by UKRI in their [research data policy]( https://www.ukri.org/publications/uk-research-and-innovation-research-data-policy/ukri-research-data-policy/).
Click here to read more on the [FAIR principles](https://www.go-fair.org/fair-principles/).

### Global Alliance for Genomics and Health (GA4GH) Task Execution Service (TES)
GA4GH is global body that has developed a suite of standards, initially to support genomic research, that have been adopted by the wider sensitive data ecosystem. A key standard is TES that provides a standard mechanism for orchestrating complex analyses across different compute environments.
Click here to read more about [TES](https://www.ga4gh.org/product/task-execution-service-tes/)  and [GA4GH](https://www.ga4gh.org).

### Observational Medical Outcomes Partnership (OMOP)
A standard that describes how health data should be grouped together in a set of common tables to form a common data model. Created and maintained by Observational Health Data Sciences and Informatics (OHDSI), this is as a global standard for real world data studies and well-adopted across the UK.
See the [OHDSI OMOP](https://www.ohdsi.org/data-standardization/) to learn more.
